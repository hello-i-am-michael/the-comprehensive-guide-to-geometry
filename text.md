# 1.1

Point – a place in a space with no shape nor size. 

Line – a point in space that extends infinitely with no thickness (no start and no end)

Vertex – where two rays meet

Vertex has to be the middle letter of the angle naming. If an angle cannot be confused, you can use the vertex.

Plane – flat surface that extends infinitely (needs 3 points to define)

Coplanar – on the same plane

# 3.7, 3.9

Parallel – ||

Transversal: A line which passes through one or more lines.
Parallel: Two lines which are the same distance from each other and never touch. (They also have the same slope or m)

Alternate Interior Angles: angles between the two crossed lines on opposite sides of the transversal.

Alternate Interior Angle Theorem: Two lines intersected by a transversal are parallel if and only if their alternate interior angles are congruent

Alternate Exterior Angles: angles between the two crossed lines on opposite sides of the transversal.

Alternate Exterior Angles Theorem: Two lines intersected by a transversal are parallel if and only if their alternate exterior angles are congruent

Corresponding Angles: angles that occur in the same position relative to the transversal and crossed lines.

Corresponding Angles Postulate: Two lines intersected by a transversal are parallel if and only if their corresponding angles are congruent.

Consecutive Angles: angles on the same side of the transversal inside or outside (both inside or both outside) the parallel lines.

Side-Side-Side (SSS) Postulate: If 3 sides of one angle are congruent to 3 sides of another, then the triangles are congruent.
Side-Angle-Side (SAS) Postulate: if two sides and the angle between one of them are congruent to two sides and the angle between one of them on another triangle, the two triangles are congruent.
Angle-Side-Angle (ASA) Postulate: If there is a side of a triangle between two angles, and another triangle has congruent parts to the two angles and the side, the two triangles are congruent.
Angle-Angle-Side (AAS) Postulate: If there is an angle, another angle, and a side next to each other congruent to another set on another triangle, the two triangles are congruent.
Hypotenuse-Leg (HL) Postulate: if the hypotenuse and leg of one right triangle is congruent to the hypotenuse and leg of a second right triangle, then the two triangles must be congruent.
